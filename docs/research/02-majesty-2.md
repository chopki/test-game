# Majesty 2: The Fantasy Kingdom Sim (2009) and expansions

1C:Ino-Co, published by Paradox (PC September 2009, Mac 2010). Expansions: *Kingmaker* (2010, random dens and
trading posts, a map generator), *Battles of Ardania* (2010), *Monster Kingdom* (2011, play as monsters), and the
unofficial *Cold Sunrise* (2011). Metacritic about 72, GameRankings about 76. This file covers **what changed from
Majesty 1** and why it matters. Numbers come from the Majesty 2 wiki (majesty2.fandom.com) and contemporary reviews.

## The big changes

| Area | Majesty 1 | Majesty 2 |
| ---- | --------- | --------- |
| Engine | 2D isometric | 3D, rotating camera |
| Maps | Quests + random **Freestyle** | Campaign missions only at launch, **no random maps** (blamed on the 3D engine); patch 1.3 and *Kingmaker* added random dens and placement plus an editor |
| Flags | Attack, Explore | **Attack, Explore, Defend, Fear** |
| Heroes per guild | 4 | **3** (temples 2) |
| Roster | Guild heroes + temple heroes + one race | **Three tiers with promotion**: guild heroes can be promoted at a temple |
| Temples | Anywhere (Palace 2/3), rival gods exclude each other | Only on fixed **Holy Ground** sites, Palace 3 |
| Marketplace | Several, with spacing rules | **One per mission** |
| Repeat building cost | Varies (×1.1 … ×4) | ×1.5 per copy for most |
| Parties | Emerge from follow behaviours | **Formed at an Inn** (2–4 heroes; you pick from those signalling "looking for group") |
| Between missions | Nothing carried | **Lords**: promote a favourite hero, summon them later from a Hall of Lords |
| Kingdom powers | Spells from temples and guild | Plus four campaign **artifacts** with big active effects |

## Flags in detail

| Flag | Pays out | Notes |
| ---- | -------- | ----- |
| Attack | Split among the interested heroes when the target dies | Units or buildings |
| Explore | Split among the interested heroes when they reach it | |
| Defend | **A share paid over time** to heroes guarding the target | Expires and must be renewed; reviewers found it the most frustrating, as heroes drift off to other flags |
| Fear | – | Keeps heroes **away**; the more gold, the higher the level of hero it scares off |

Bounties go up in 100 or 500 steps, with a settable default. Reviewers noted that heroes take a flag only "if the
bounty is to their liking", the same weighing as Majesty 1's greed × reward.

**Lesson:** Defend and Fear are the two ideas worth keeping. Defend gives the player a positional lever, but paying
it out over time and making it expire turns it into upkeep. Fear is an elegant **negative incentive**.

## Heroes: tiers and promotion

| Tier | Class | Cost | Home | Promotes to |
| ---- | ----- | ---: | ---- | ----------- |
| 1 | Rogue | 100 | Rogues' Guild | Assassin (Lunord) |
| 1 | Ranger | 150 | Rangers' Guild | Beastmaster (Fervus) or Archer of Helia |
| 1 | Warrior | 200 | Warriors' Guild | Paladin (Dauros) or Blademaster (Krolm) |
| 1 | Cleric | 350 | Clerics' Guild | Sister of Krypta or Priestess of Agrela |
| 1 | Mage | 500 | Wizards' Guild | Ice Mage (Temple of Ice) |
| 2 | Dwarf / Elf | 700 | Dwarven Settlement / Elven Bungalow | – |
| 3 | Temple heroes | 1000 | Their temple (2 each) | – |

*Monster Kingdom* swaps in goblins (spearman 200, archer 150), ratman robbers (100), liches (500), koatl and minotaurs
(700), noble werewolves (1000) and Grum-Gog temple heroes.

Promotion keeps a cherished hero alive as a stronger class, and it ties the temple choice to the heroes you already
have, which is a nicer upgrade path than "recruit a new class".

**Tax collectors**: 4 / 5 / 6 by Palace level (35 HP). By default they visit buildings holding 10+ gold and go home
once carrying 25+, and you can change both. A **homeless hero waits 10 days** in the Palace for a new home, then
leaves your service.

## Equipment: research is the player's sink, purchase is cheap

| Tier | Your research | Hero pays | Warrior weapon | Mage weapon | Elf bow | Warrior armour (melee/ranged/magic) |
| ---- | ------------: | --------: | -------------: | ----------: | ------: | ----------------------------------- |
| 1 | 250 | 30 | +4 | +11 | +11 | 35 / 20 / 5 |
| 2 | 1000 | 70 | +10 | +34 | +32 | 55 / 35 / 10 |
| 3 | 3000 | 100 | +20 | +65 | +60 | 75 / 50 / 25 |
| 4 | 3500 | 120 | +35 | +100 | +95 | 95 / 60 / 35 |

Compared with Majesty 1 (research 200–400, a hero pays 100–900), Majesty 2 moved the cost onto **your research** and
made hero purchases cheap. Heroes upgrade quickly once you've paid, but **sales tax stops being a big income**. A hero
only uses a blacksmith that has researched the item it wants, so a second smith without the research is worthless.

## What reviewers said

- **Repetition.** "Repetitive, formulaic missions" (GameSpot 6.5). "Developing your heroes and coaxing them around soon
  starts to feel like a chore" (IT Reviews). No freestyle mode at launch (IncGamers), no co-op.
- **Fewer decisions.** One review argues the sequel removed choices: in Majesty 1, three level-1 marketplaces against
  one level-3 market was a real decision. Rising repeat costs, a single marketplace and fixed temple sites point
  every player to the same small, high-level town.
- PC Gamer UK: "too many malfunctions to justify its existence" (bugs and AI quirks).
- The 3D camera and the party system were generally welcomed.

## Takeaways

1. **Random maps and a freestyle mode are core features.** Majesty 2's biggest complaint follows directly from
   dropping them.
2. **Don't funnel builds.** Unique-building limits and fixed sites remove the spatial puzzle.
3. **Keep:** Defend and Fear flags (simplified), party formation as a deliberate tool, promotion trees, Lords as light
   meta-progression, and research-then-buy item tiers.
4. **Balance research against sales tax** on purpose. Who pays for gear decides whether heroes are an income source.
