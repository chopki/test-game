# Rule the Realm from a living chart

Realm should look like **a ruler's chart of the kingdom that gets drawn and written as you play**. The known land is inked and painted. The unknown is blank vellum with a cartographer's guesses on it. Heroes are painted figures standing on coloured bases, bounties are sealed notices pinned to the map, and the hero barks and the chronicle are lettered by a royal scribe. At night the table falls dark except for lantern light. None of the twelve reference games, and none of the phone strategy games surveyed, presents its world this way. Their looks fall into four groups: lit dioramas (Majesty 2, Crown of Greed, Thronefall, Against the Storm), pixel strips and pixel towns (Kingdom, Dungeon Village), outlined cartoons (Kingdom Rush), and god-hand or data-panel interfaces (Black & White, Dungeon Keeper, RimWorld, Stronghold). Not one of them shows the world as a document. The direction also costs little, because Hold the Hall already has most of the parts. The sprites get their ink at bake time, the ground is a pale painted surface with a tile grid, the UI is parchment and iron, and fog is already a cheap shader over a small chunk mask. Most of the first stage is recolouring, restyling and rearranging DOM, with no re-bake. The phone evidence matters as much as the art. Majesty's own phone port, Crown of Greed and Dungeon Village were each faulted for tiny text, main actions buried in menus, or pop-ups that block play. The parts of the identity that carry information (seals, banderoles, bases, the chart) therefore have to be built around large, labelled, non-blocking touch targets. One caveat applies throughout: the research was done from text sources only. Many visual details of the reference games (flag colours, HUD geometry, palettes) could not be checked against screenshots and are left out or flagged as unverified.

## Twelve games, four looks, and an empty corner

The **Majesty line** splits by medium. Majesty 1 is 2D isometric sprites, and Majesty 2 moved the same game into 3D ([GameBanshee preview](https://www.gamebanshee.com/xxyr)). Reviewers liked the original's "tiny details in building animations and inspired visual flourishes" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/027948/r/)). Majesty 2 was "colorful and fun to watch", and its interface was rebuilt around **build queues, a pause button and hero portraits** for finding the autonomous heroes ([GameBanshee review](http://www.gamebanshee.com/kakyu)). Crown of Greed, released on 31 March 2026, goes for a "gloomy and dark" Slavic-folklore 3D look ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1697870/)) with buildings that look alive: lights switch on and off and damage shows. Its UI is the genre's most recent warning. Playtesters said bounties and buildings were "hidden" in a castle panel, spells took several steps through a wheel, and the UI was "adjustable for console control" but "not good for strategy on PC" ([Steam forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1697870/discussions/0/595139814470240895)). After release, players still found **flag icons on the minimap hard to see** ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1697870/crown_of_greed.md)), and critics noted it has no visible day/night clock and no crisis pings ([Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/gold-instead-of-obedience-crown-of-greed-tested-42293)). Steam reviews sit at Mixed, 59% positive ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1697870/)). Kairosoft's Dungeon Village is bright, chunky pixel art. Its villagers chatter in speech bubbles and quest progress runs along a bottom bar, but its best-documented trait is **"an over-abundance of informational pop-ups"** that pause the game ([LadiesGamers](https://ladiesgamers.com/dungeon-village-2-review/)). That was the top negative theme in player reviews of the sequel ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1983710/dungeon_village_2.md)). Across the whole line, the strongest channel for hero personality has been **voice**: Majesty's royal advisor, George Ledoux, "was one of the highlights" ([GameBanshee](http://www.gamebanshee.com/kakyu)).

The **three "Kingdom" games** each own a medium outright. Kingdom is side-on "modern pixel art", and its interface is almost entirely diegetic: coins are physical objects, and there is close to no HUD ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)). On phones it went fully gestural. Kingdom Two Crowns "doesn't have a single onscreen button", and a reviewer complained that it "never once told me to swipe up" ([AppUnwrapper](https://www.appunwrapper.com/2020/05/17/kingdom-two-crowns-ios-review/)). Thronefall is a flat-shaded, low-poly toy diorama with "a very distinct colour palette" per level. Its readability comes from previewing each night's spawn points with **melee, ranged and flying icons and counts**: "you always have the information you need" ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)). It is 95% positive across more than 11,000 Steam reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150/Thronefall/)). Its sparse UI still needed a mobile update that added tower-range display, damage numbers and unit stats ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thronefall-a-little-kingdom/id6744029320)). Kingdom Rush is thick-outlined cartoon art with "clear and memorable" icons, round thumb-sized buttons and a **second tap to confirm a purchase**, and tapping elsewhere cancels without spending ([Emily Miles](https://emilym.space/thumbelina-hurts-mobile-ui-blog/2018/6/26/kingdom-rush-a-tower-defense-trilogy-with-ui-design-approaching-perfection-and-entertainment-worth-missing-bedtime-for)). Its wave is called from an icon at the mouth of the path with a circular timer ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/2014-07-30-kingdom-rush-tips-and-tricks.html)). The series passed 100M downloads ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)), but its sprites are "rather small" on phones ([CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-vengeance-mobile-review)).

The **god games and colony sims** make the player's presence, or the world's mood, the signature rather than the interface frame. Black & White was built "with the intention of having the main user interface free of icons, buttons, and panels" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))). Its standout device is the **village-store flag that rises as need grows**, while villagers shout "Worshippers need food!" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/playing-god-the-revolutionary-ai-that-made-black-and-white-still-unforgettable/)). Dungeon Keeper uses the Hand of Evil as its cursor and a sardonic Mentor as its alert system: "Your imps are overworked" ([ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/c-the-mentor)). DK2's interface was **redesigned more than ten times** before the public could follow it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)). Stronghold shows realm mood through a character: the Scribe's book carries the popularity number ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/40970/discussions/0/3147430975647502052/)). Critics have called its panel art "a bit bland" ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition-review/)). RimWorld pushes the drama into text and data. It has a colonist portrait bar, colour-coded **event letters (blue good, grey neutral, yellow bad, red threat)** down the right edge ([RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events)), and a mood bar with threshold ticks. The ticks were hard enough to read at a glance that version 1.5 made portraits **glow below break thresholds** ([RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Version/1.5.4062)). Against the Storm puts a season clock and a red Hostility "eye" at top centre ([Fextralife](https://againstthestorm.wiki.fextralife.com/Against_the_Storm_Hostility)) and grades its painterly world by season. Players say it "will always tell you why your villagers are happy or unhappy but does a poor job explaining how" to fix it ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4286936944588739641/)). Some of its text is small or low-contrast ([Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Against+the+Storm)).

The table below sets out what each game "owns" visually, which is what Realm must not imitate. It is a synthesis of the sources above; cells drawn from unverified recall are kept out.

| Game | Owns this look | Owns this UI device | Realm's lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Majesty 1 / HD / mobile | Busy 2D isometric sprite town | Voiced royal advisor | Personality through words; PC text shrunk onto phones fails |
| Majesty 2 | Colourful, toy-like 3D | Hero portraits, pause, build queues | Players need a portrait roster to find autonomous heroes |
| Crown of Greed | Gloomy Slavic-folklore 3D, living buildings | Bounty "contracts", rally flag | Never bury flags behind a panel or wheel |
| Dungeon Village | Saturated pixel village | Speech bubbles, bottom quest bar | No blocking pop-ups |
| Kingdom | Side-on pixel art, moody light | Coins as objects, no HUD | Diegetic is lovely; hidden gestures are not |
| Thronefall | Flat low-poly diorama | Spawn previews with type icons | Show the threat before night |
| Kingdom Rush | Thick-outlined cartoon | Build rings, tap-twice confirm, skull timer | Confirm before spending; keep popups away from tappable spots |
| Black & White | Lush island; world changes with alignment | God hand, rising store flags, shouts | Show state in the world |
| Dungeon Keeper | Torch-lit dungeon, dark comedy | Hand of Evil, Mentor voice | Alerts with personality |
| Stronghold | Sunlit isometric castle-building | Scribe as a living meter | A character can be the gauge |

| Against the Storm | Painterly, weather-graded | Season clock + Hostility eye, top centre | Say why and how to fix it |

The empty corner is clear. Every game above presents a lit diorama, a 2D strip or a data panel. **None presents the realm as a document**: a map being drawn, a chronicle being written. Researchers in two separate notes reached the same finding independently.

## Phones punish small text, buried verbs and blocking pop-ups

The phone evidence agrees with itself more than the art evidence does. Majesty's 2011 iOS port was judged a good fit for touch in principle, because bounty play carries no clicks-per-minute pressure. In practice, positioning "buildings and waypoints is occasionally troublesome, as is the targeting of your spells", spells were "denoted by icon only", and text was "much trickier to decipher on an iPhone screen" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2011/08/18/majesty-the-fantasy-kingdom-sim-review)). Kingdom New Lands sometimes "mistakes movement requests for the redistribution of wealth" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review)), so a swipe ends up spending gold. Kingdom Rush's extra-weapons menu "frequently rolls out at inappropriate times" because it sits near a tower slot, and its hero icon is easy to press by accident ([Emily Miles](https://emilym.space/thumbelina-hurts-mobile-ui-blog/2018/6/26/kingdom-rush-a-tower-defense-trilogy-with-ui-design-approaching-perfection-and-entertainment-worth-missing-bedtime-for)). On a small screen, Cult of the Lamb's text box "felt very large, going over the creature's head" ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/cult-of-the-lamb/review/)), which is a direct warning for hero barks. Kingdom Rush Origins makes heroes hard to track in big fights on a small screen ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2014/12/19/kingdom-rush-origins-review/)). For Kairosoft's Kingdom Adventurers, the top complaint one aggregator lists is heroes' roundabout pathing ([game-solver](https://game-solver.com/kingdom-adventurers/), methodology unverified). Both point the same way: when units act on their own, players watch where they go and want to see why.

The patterns that work are just as consistent. Kingdom Rush uses fixed slots, a contextual ring and a confirm tap. Bad North uses "tap the troop unit and then tap where you want them to go", with time slowed during combat ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/bad-north-shows-that-even-bloody-viking-battles-can-be-artsy-and-cute/)). Townscaper uses tap to place and hold to delete ([AppUnwrapper](https://www.appunwrapper.com/2021/10/20/townscaper-ios-review/)). Clash of Clans' October 2026 revamp puts the two core verbs in **mirrored bottom corners** and shows a notification as **a badge on the button it concerns**, with a red pip only "when there's a real reason to visit" that clears once visited ([Supercell](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashofclans/blog/news/weve-revamped-the-clash-of-clans-user-interface/)). RimWorld's console edition anchors its UI to the four corners so that "you're never more than three button presses away" ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/rimworld-console-edition-review-ps4-xbox-one/)). Thumb-zone research builds on Steven Hoober's field study, in which 49% of people held the phone one-handed; it puts easy reach at the bottom of the screen and hard reach in the top corners ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2016/09/the-thumb-zone-designing-for-mobile-users)). When Brawl Stars' camera zoomed out, Supercell drew "all characters bigger too" so they stayed readable ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2018/03/21/brawl-stars-landscape-balance-update-attempts-to-improve-long-range-brawlers/)).

Hold the Hall already follows several of these patterns. Bounty flags sit in the build wheel, one tap from the main screen ([HUD.js](../src/ui/HUD.js)). Placement uses a drag plus ✓/✕ confirm. The minimap marks found camps and flags ([Minimap.js](../src/ui/Minimap.js)). A flag's card lists who took it and who turned it down, and why ([RealmPanel.js](../src/ui/RealmPanel.js)). That last feature goes further than anything documented for the Majesty line, where no source describes a "who took it" display. The gaps lie elsewhere. On a phone, the Realm wheel overflows into two arcs because guilds, the Smithy and the bounty all share it. Heroes are the enemy soldier sprites re-tinted red and given crowns (the renderer treats `realm === 'hero'` as elite, and their portraits are drawn from `spriteKeys.enemy(..., 'red')`), so they are told apart by colour alone. Barks are plain "Name: text" pills. On small screens, Realm news arrives only as toasts that vanish.

## The ruler's table is the open position

The most recognisable indie identities share one trait: a **single governing metaphor that every system obeys**. Inkulinati's battlefield is a manuscript page, and its medievalist consultant checks that the mechanics fit the meaning of the marginalia ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2019/10/31/playing-with-history-illuminating-the-insane-game-world-of-inkulinati/)). Pentiment's **fonts are the character voice**, encoding each speaker's education and social standing ([Lettermatic](https://lettermatic.com/custom/pentiment)). Tunic's manual is a physical booklet whose pages are found out of order and make sense later ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/how-tunics-cool-in-game-manual-was-made/1100-6507719/)). Sable's distance fog "provides the framework for the day/night cycle" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Darkest Dungeon keeps black "everywhere except the inks" out of the palette and yellows even its cool colours toward worn parchment ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com:443/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/)). Cult of the Lamb sets a safe-feeling base against a creepier forest ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/cult-of-the-lamb-massive-monster-take-dungeon-crawling-and-base-building-to-new-levels/)).

The metaphor that fits a Majesty-like is **the ruler at a table, not the commander in the field**. In Majesty you never command, you post rewards, so a presentation that makes the player's distance part of the fiction suits the mechanics the way Inkulinati's consultant asks of its art. A chart fits Realm's existing systems unusually well. Fog becomes unwritten land. Exploring becomes the act of drawing the map. Bounties become notices pinned to it. Heroes become pieces moving across it on their own. The chronicle becomes the scribe's record. Hold the Hall's phone reference already points this way. Its ground is pale, almost paper-coloured, with a faint tile grid, under parchment-and-iron trim ([ui-hud-phone.jpg](../docs/reference/ui-hud-phone.jpg)). The battlefield already looks halfway to a drawn map.

Three objections deserve an answer. The first is that the look sits close to Hold the Hall's own Survival mode, so Realm could read as "the tower-defence game with fog". The answer is to commit to the devices that only Realm has (figure bases, seals, vellum fog, lettered barks) rather than to a new render style. The second is that a paper-toned field could lose contrast in busy fights. Nobody has tested whether a map-like presentation dulls combat on phones, so night lighting and figure bases have to carry the contrast. The third is that Inkulinati and Pentiment already own the "manuscript" among indie players. Within the Majesty-like and phone-strategy space, though, the position is empty, and a cartographer's chart is not a manuscript page. Concepts that need a full re-bake and push readability harder are possible: a woodcut style that thickens the ink and hatching, or a papercraft pop-up book. Both cost far more and are best kept as later options.

## Recommended direction: chart, chronicle and lantern

The direction combines three layers, each built on technology the game already has.

**The Chart is the world.** The painted ground and the inked sprites stay as they are. Their ink is baked onto the CPU at bake time: a warm brown outline `[44,30,22]`, four shading bands and hatching at 0.12 ([inkStyle.js](../src/render/inkStyle.js)), so the look costs nothing at runtime. The fog stops being a grey-violet cloud (`mix(vec3(0.17,0.16,0.19), vec3(0.33,0.31,0.33), n)` in [realmLayer.js](../src/render/realmLayer.js)) and becomes **blank vellum**, revealed with an ink-bleed edge. A faint world-anchored paper grain can sit under the whole map. Heroes stand on painted bases like pieces on a map.

**The Chronicle is the voice.** Hold the Hall has no audio yet, so the genre's strongest channel, the voiced advisor, is out of reach. Pentiment shows the substitute: **lettering as voice**. Barks become short speech banderoles (scroll ribbons) above heroes, set in a hand that matches the hero's class. Barks and deeds are copied into the chronicle, whose entries carry **wax seals in RimWorld's four severity tiers**. On phones, the chronicle becomes the alert surface rather than disappearing toasts.

**The Lantern is the night.** The sprites' lighting is baked in and cannot change, so night has to come from a colour grade plus local light, as Sable does with fog. Day keeps the existing warm CSS grade (`sepia(0.08) saturate(1.04) contrast(1.04)` in [Renderer.js](../src/render/Renderer.js)). Night darkens the field and turns the vellum fog to indigo ink wash. Lantern pools (the High setting's existing glows) mark the Hall, torches, camps and each hero.

This direction rules out a real-time outline (Sobel) pass. A WebGL Moebius-style post-process needs **two extra full-scene renders plus about 18 texture samples per pixel** ([Maxime Heckel](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/moebius-style-post-processing)), and it would draw a second line over sprites that already carry ink. It also rules out 1-bit dithering, which shimmers under camera motion unless stabilised; Lucas Pope's own lesson was to "use dithering as little as possible" ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2019/10/17/lucas-pope-on-return-of-the-obra-dinns-art-style)). For fog, a map-sized render target was a large slowdown on phones. A small lookup texture updated only on change is the recommended pattern ([html5gamedevs](https://html5gamedevs.com/topic/38537-fog-of-war-performance)), and it is exactly what `realmLayer.js` already does: a chunk-resolution `DataTexture` re-uploaded only when `fog.version` changes.

## Device-by-device proposals

### Heroes: painted figures on coloured bases

Heroes need to read as *ours, autonomous and individual* without new models at first. Each hero gets a **round base decal** under its feet, drawn through the existing ground-marks batch. The base is a ring in the guild's colour: madder red for men-at-arms, verdigris green for rangers, saffron for rogues. Blue belongs to the enemy army's livery and grey to the outlaws, so neither is used. A health arc runs round the ring and level pips sit on its rim. The base also gives a large, stable tap target, which helps against the "hard to track heroes" problem. Draw heroes about 10–15% larger than foes, as Brawl Stars did when its camera pulled back. Above the head, show a small **intent glyph** when the hero is not fighting: a coin for greed, a pennant for a bounty, a roof for going home, a boot for fleeing. This turns `intentOf` (currently text on the hero card) into something visible on the map, answering the pathing complaints by showing purpose. A selected hero gets a brass name pin. Later, after a re-bake, class silhouettes should replace re-tinted soldiers: a hooded ranger with a longbow, a cloaked rogue, a man-at-arms in a tabard in his guild's colour. Colour alone is the weakest way to tell units apart at phone size, as the Thronefall reviewers who found enemies "sometimes lack clarity" suggest ([iPhonesoft](https://iphonesoft.fr/2025/05/21/excellent-thronefall-defendre-royaume-ios)).

### Bounty flags: sealed notices you can read at a glance

The flag stays a pole with a pennant in the world, but each kind gets its own **shape** as well as its own colour, following Mini Metro's shape coding ([Next City](https://nextcity.org/daily/entry/mini-metro-game-transportation-planning)). Attack is a swallow-tailed red pennant with a sword. Explore is a square cream pennant with a compass rose. Defend (a shield) and Fear arrive later. The DOM gold pill above the pole becomes a **wax seal stamped with the amount**, and a small coin stack at the foot of the pole grows in steps (25, 50, 100+) as the bounty is raised. Takers show up twice. On the map, each taker's guild colour appears as a ribbon tied to the pole. On the tag, small portrait pips show who has taken it. Turned-down heroes stay on the flag card, where their reasons already live, with one added line on **how to change their mind** ("Bram wants 50+ to dare this camp"). This answers Against the Storm's "tells you why, not how" complaint. Placement keeps the snap-and-confirm flow: pick the flag kind, tap a camp, foe or ground (it snaps, with a preview ring), then ✓ with the cost shown. Placing a flag must never share a gesture with panning. On the minimap, flags should be drawn as the brightest marks, because Crown of Greed's faint minimap flags drew complaints.

### Fog of war: unwritten vellum and the cartographer's guesses

Recolour the fog shader to warm paper tones (unexplored land as pale vellum with a faint deckled pattern). Darken the noise threshold's edge into an **ink-bleed rim**, so revealed land looks freshly drawn rather than cleared of smoke. This is a change to constants and a few lines of GLSL in `realmLayer.js`, with no new texture. In a second step, add **cartographer's marks** inside unseen chunks: dashed roads that run on past the known edge, hatched hill symbols, and a hand-drawn "?" skull where rumour places a camp. The `lairRose` event already reports "Word is the outlaws have a new camp out in the wilds", so a rough area mark is a small step. A later sepia "stale" state for land seen but no longer watched would need the sim to record when each chunk was last seen. That change belongs in `src/sim/realm/fog.js` with tests, and it must not be added in the renderer. At night the vellum turns to indigo ink wash, so the fog itself carries the day/night shift.

### Barks: lettered banderoles that never hide the hero

Today a bark is a "Name: text" pill held for 3.5 s. Restyle it as a **curling parchment banderole**: one line of about 28 characters, a pointer at the head, and the guild colour on the ribbon's ends in place of the name. Position it offset to one side so it never covers the speaker or the object the player just tapped, which was Cult of the Lamb's problem. Give each class its own hand: a firm blackletter-style face for men-at-arms, an open chancery hand for rangers, a cramped scrawl for rogues. Fonts must be subset and come with a **plain-font option**, because Pentiment's calligraphy was hard for some readers ([Well Played](https://www.well-played.com.au/pentiment-preview-a-murder-mystery-scratched-in-ink/)). Numbers and stats always use the plain UI face. Limit barks on a phone to about three at a time, one per hero, with priority given to danger, then bounty, then greed, then idle chatter. Each bark that matters, such as taking a bounty, fleeing or a level-up, is copied into the chronicle as a quotation. This keeps Kairosoft's charm of chattering heroes, the "Worshippers need food!" spirit of Black & White, and Majesty's humour, without Kingdom Rush's voiced pop-culture quips.

### Day and night: table light, a dusk herald and a dawn page

Three moments need telegraphing, and the best games layer a persistent clock, a change in the world, and a cue at the transition. In Realm, the top-right wave dial becomes a **sun-and-moon roundel** in the style of an illuminated medallion. That gives Realm the visible day clock Crown of Greed lacks. At **dusk**, a bell sounds (once audio exists) and the dagger-ended ribbon from the phone reference announces the raid ("Dusk: the blue army gathers to the east"). Arrowed pennants at the map edges preview each side, using the threat-preview pattern Thronefall reviewers praised. At **night**, a cool multiply grade and vignette go over the field through the existing CSS-filter path, glows mark the Hall, torches and camps, and each hero carries a small light radius so its position never disappears. A low-resolution light map can come later if the CSS route costs too much compositing time on mobile browsers, which nobody has yet measured. At **dawn**, the chronicle opens a non-blocking "dawn page" for that night: gold taken, heroes lost and levelled, what stirred in the wilds. The game keeps running under it, and a tap dismisses it.

### Chronicle and notifications: one alert surface, sealed by severity

Every Realm event becomes a chronicle entry with a **wax seal** coloured by severity: gold for good, grey for neutral, amber for bad, red for a threat. These are RimWorld's proven four tiers in Realm's own material. On phones, replace vanishing toasts with a **herald ribbon** at top centre that shows the latest entry. Tapping it unrolls the chronicle as a bottom sheet, and tapping an entry moves the camera to its subject, the way RimWorld's letters jump to theirs. Threats off screen get an **edge ping**, an arrow sealed in the threat's colour pointing toward a raid, a besieged guild or a falling hero. Use Clash of Clans' badge rule: a guild's medallion shows a pip only when a hero can actually be hired there, and the pip clears once seen. Repeated events should be merged ("Three heroes bought steel gear"). Nothing ever pauses the game or blocks input. That one rule answers Dungeon Village's most cited flaw.

### Hero, camp and flag cards: documents, not data sheets

Cards stay as bottom sheets, and the camera nudges the subject into the upper half so a card never covers what it describes. The **hero card** reads like a commission. A portrait sits in a guild-coloured roundel, the name is set in the hero's class lettering, and the trait appears as a ribbon with its effect in plain words ("Craven: runs at 45% health"). Below that come intent with its reason, gold carried, gear, kills and a health bar. Each recent refusal or worry is paired with a hint for the player. The **camp card** is a broadside "wanted" notice: camp level, guards, time to its next raid, loot on offer, and a single large "Post a bounty" seal button that starts a flag on the camp. The **flag card** is the notice itself: the seal and its amount, +25 and +100 steppers sized for a thumb, takers as ribbons, and refusals with their reasons and fixes. Text must have a minimum size; the notes suggest 14–16 px for body text. Ornament can surround the text but must never sit behind it at reduced contrast. Small or low-contrast text was the most common readability complaint across Majesty mobile, Against the Storm and RimWorld's console edition.

### Phone HUD layout and touch patterns

Design the portrait layout first: the phone reference is portrait, and indirect control (no steering) is exactly where portrait works. Rarely tapped information goes in the hard-to-reach top band. That means the Hall ring and gold at top left, the sun-and-moon roundel at top right with settings under it, and the herald ribbon across the top centre. The two core verbs go in **mirrored bottom corners**, following the Clash of Clans revamp. The hammer fan stays bottom right for building and hiring (guilds, Smithy, walls, towers). A new **Decree seal** at bottom left fans out the flags (Attack, Explore, then Defend and Fear) and later the spells. This splits up the two-arc wheel that crowds phones today and keeps every flag one tap from the main screen, which was Crown of Greed's lesson. The time controls (pause, speed, ring the night bell early) shrink to a short row above the Decree seal. A **hero rail** of portrait roundels runs up the left edge, low enough for the left thumb. Each roundel has a health ring and an intent badge. Tapping one centres the camera and opens the card, a second tap follows the hero, and the rail lists idle or hurt heroes first. For touch, a tap selects the nearest hero within a generous radius. Tapping a crowd fans out its portraits so the player can pick one. A long press opens a card, replacing desktop hover tooltips. Every spend uses select, then target, then ✓. Every gesture has a visible affordance. Touch targets are at least 44 pt (Apple's guideline as given in the notes, not checked against the source). The browser vibration API is unreliable on iOS, so haptics can only be a bonus.

## What to avoid, and who already owns it

Avoid **thick black outlines and comic hit lettering**: that is Kingdom Rush's territory, and its warm palette and parchment menus are already the closest overlap with Hold the Hall. Avoid **flat low-poly, blue-against-red toy dioramas, and making the day-build/night-fight rhythm the headline**: that belongs to Thronefall. Avoid a **blue-grey storm wash and a numeric Hostility eye**, which are Against the Storm's. Do not use a **god-hand cursor**, which belongs to Black & White and Dungeon Keeper, or a **narrator portrait as the meter**, which belongs to Stronghold. Avoid **pixel art**, which belongs to Kingdom and Kairosoft, and **grim desaturated folklore**, which belongs to Crown of Greed. On the interaction side, avoid modal or unskippable pop-ups, console-style wheels that hide flags, icon-only spells, script fonts for numbers, gestures with no on-screen prompt, one gesture that both pans and spends, text boxes over the speaker, and menus that open beside other tappable world spots. On the rendering side, avoid a runtime outline post-process, dithering, and any full-map render target for fog or light. Avoid Dungeon Keeper mobile's tone as well. Its downfall was timers and gems wrapped around a beloved look ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_(2014_video_game))), not its touch UI.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which art style?" to "which object is the player looking at?" Each successful game in the set answers that with a single metaphor, whether coins, toys, cartoons, a god's hand or a data panel. Hold the Hall's baked ink, pale gridded ground and parchment UI already describe an object nobody else in the genre has chosen: a chart that is still being drawn. Realm's mechanics also explain themselves better in that frame than in any other. Fog becomes unwritten land, bounties become pinned notices, and refusals become notes in the margin. The game's most distinctive feature, showing who took a bounty and why the others refused, turns into the core of its look rather than a line on a card.

The main uncertainty is visual proof. Nobody has checked these games against screenshots, and nobody has tested whether a paper-toned field keeps fights readable on a sunlit phone. The staged order covers that risk: the first two stages are cheap and reversible and target readability, and they should be put in front of players before any re-bake commits the art. Treat the night lighting as the deciding test. If heroes stay readable by lantern light on a mid-range phone, the direction works.
