# God games, castle builders and colony sims: UI, HUD and art direction (DK1/2 + War for the Overworld, Black & White 1/2, Stronghold series, RimWorld, Against the Storm)

Scope note: research for a Majesty-style "Realm" mode in Hold the Hall (phone-first, isometric, painted storybook sprites, fan build wheel bottom-right, parchment/bronze plaques, chronicle log, autonomous heroes with traits/moods/barks, bounty flags, fog, day/night waves). Economy mechanics are out of scope except where they show up as UI.
Source quality: official primary sources (Ludeon blog, Eremite devlogs, Bullfrog/Lionhead postmortems) were mostly unreachable (403/402 on the Hooded Horse wiki, Fandom wikis, GameFAQs, TechRaptor). The sources used are mainly the RimWorld Wiki (community but closely tracks the game), Wikipedia, published reviews, GamesRadar retrospectives, fan wikis and guides, and Steam threads. Each item is labelled where confidence is low.

## 1. Art direction and signature visual devices (palette, perspective, unit/state readability)

### Takeaway
All seven games make the player's "presence" or the world's mood the signature, not the chrome. Two things stand out: DK/B&W's in-world hand, and B&W's alignment-driven world that changes the land, the interface, the music and the creature. Against the Storm's signature is weather tinting a painterly/cel-shaded world. RimWorld goes the other way, with functional minimalism that pushes the drama into text. Stronghold's identity is mostly its sprite castle-building plus a human narrator (the Scribe), not the panel art.

### Cited Findings
**Black & White (2001) / B&W2 (2005)**
- B&W was written from scratch "with the intention of having the main user interface free of icons, buttons, and panels"; an animated on-screen hand throws people and objects, taps houses to wake occupants, and casts miracles — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- Artist Mark Healey wanted the cursor to be a magician's hand — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- Land, interface, music and the Hand itself change with the player's good/evil alignment. A good god's Temple is brightly coloured and an evil god's is built to intimidate. Evil wolves get glowing eyes, large fangs and claws, while good wolves turn purple and glow softly. The creature morphs between alignment forms and fatness levels, and its eyes are separate geometry that moves independently — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- Creatures are anthropomorphised bipedal animals, chosen because the humanoid form "evoked empathy and suggested sentience". Temples are holdovers from a "medieval, fairy-tale" wizard-citadel concept. Every Lionhead staff member's face was digitised for villagers — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- GamesRadar (2026 retrospective) calls the front end "remarkably close" to HUDless, with gesture prompts appearing bottom-right later in the game. It describes the villagers as "quintessentially British", speaking "in regional accents", with "chocolate-box charm" — [GamesRadar, Phil Iwaniuk](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/playing-god-the-revolutionary-ai-that-made-black-and-white-still-unforgettable/)
- Reviewers: IGN (Tal Blevins) "a visual masterpiece"; CGW (Charles Ardai) called the landscape "stunning"; PC Gamer (Li C. Kuo) "awesome" interface, "beautiful" graphics — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- B&W2 uses "a more conventional heads-up display", but is still "almost button-less": mainly the world, the hand, and toolbars that can be hidden — [Wikipedia: Black & White 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_2)

**Dungeon Keeper 1 (1997) / DK2 (1999) / War for the Overworld (2015)**
- The Hand of Evil is the cursor. It picks up and drops creatures (8 at a time in DK1; a fan wiki says up to 64 in DK2). Slapping makes a creature work harder but costs it health — [Dungeon Keeper Wiki: Hand of Evil](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Hand_of_Evil); [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)
- In DK2 every creature except Skeletons gains 0.5 annoyance per second while held (fan wiki; unverified) — [Dungeon Keeper Wiki: Hand of Evil](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Hand_of_Evil)
- Possession: the player can possess a creature and play from its perspective — [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)
- DK2 moved to hardware-accelerated 3D with DirectX lighting effects. IGN (Trent C. Ward) called the graphics "amazing", and Gamezilla (Chad Foster) praised the "incredible" detail and "realistic" sounds — [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)
- The DK1 interface is a bar on the left side of the screen for picking rooms and spells, with a minimap top-left — [Giant Bomb: Dungeon Keeper](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Dungeon_Keeper); [Computer Hope](https://www.computerhope.com:443/games/games/dungeon.htm)
- War for the Overworld is billed as a spiritual successor. A hands-on said DK veterans "will feel right at home with the look and interface". The developers admitted the hand cursor was "quite janky right now" (launch era) — [GamingBolt hands-on](https://gamingbolt.com/war-for-the-overworld-hands-on-impressions/amp); [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/pc/war-for-the-overworld)

**Stronghold (2001), Crusader (2002), Definitive Editions (2023 / 2025)**
- Original-era graphics were seen as functional: GameSpot (Ron Dulin) "The buildings look good, but not great… The animations are well done"; IGN (Dan Adams) "This isn't the prettiest game ever by a longshot"; GameZone praised "wonderful" environments — [Wikipedia: Stronghold (2001)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stronghold_(2001_video_game))
- Crusader DE: Game8 says the "UI is also much cleaner". GameGrin calls the in-game UI "a bit bland". The Outer Haven says menus and character/building designs were revamped — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition-review); [GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition-review/); [The Outer Haven](https://www.theouterhaven.net/stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition-review/)
- The original voice actors re-recorded lines for units, the Scribe and the narrator in Crusader DE — [Gamer Escape](https://gamerescape.com/2025/07/14/review-stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition/)

**RimWorld**
- Mood is "a blue bar in the Needs tab ranging from 0 to 100%". A white triangle under the bar marks the "Mood Target", and three dividing lines mark break thresholds (5% extreme, 20% major, 35% minor, shifted by traits) — [RimWorld Wiki: Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood)
- I found no sourced material on RimWorld's top-down "Prison Architect"-style limbless pawn art or its readability rationale (see Gaps).

**Against the Storm (Eremite Games, 2023 1.0)**
- Reviewers describe a "unique cell-shaded art style" (compared with older Dota / early League of Legends) and a "stunning painterly art style". A third says the art and soundtrack feel "mystical and cozy despite the looming Blightstorm" — summarised via search snippets from [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/against-storm-ambient-roguelite-city-builder), [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/against-storm-review-the-perfect-blend-genres), [MKAU Gaming](https://www.mkaugaming.com/all-review-list/against-the-storm-playstation-5-review/) (full texts blocked; attribution of each phrase to each outlet is approximate)
- Art direction drew partly on the cover of Brandon Sanderson's *The Way of Kings*, to portray a threatening storm. Graphic designer Damian Ziomek made most of the visual assets — reported via search summary of [Against the Storm Wiki: Updates and Devlog](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Updates_and_Devlog) (page 403 on direct fetch; unverified)
- A rendering-pipeline update added more real-time lighting and "readjusted post processes in all seasons in all biomes", meaning each season has its own grade — same source, same caveat
- Day is "vibrant" and night "dark and dimly illuminated" — [MKAU Gaming PS5 review](https://www.mkaugaming.com/all-review-list/against-the-storm-playstation-5-review/) (via snippet)

### Inferences
- The strongest device for us to borrow in spirit, but not copy, is B&W's "the world changes to show state". We could tint the Realm (banners, light, hero sprite variants) by kingdom prosperity or fear. The hand and the alignment-morph are too strongly owned by B&W/DK to reuse directly.
- Stronghold's look is the closest to our medieval sprite style, but critics found the UI "bland". Our framed parchment/bronze plaques already go further and should keep their ornament rather than flatten into Stronghold DE's cleaner bars.
- Against the Storm owns "painterly world plus weather grading". If our day/night waves use colour grading, a warm storybook dusk with ink-line night is safer than ATS's blue-grey storm wash.

### Gaps
- No source found on RimWorld's art rationale (limbless pawns, "Prison Architect" heritage, Tynan Sylvester on readability). The Ludeon blog wasn't fetched. From memory (unverified): RimWorld pawns are a head and body drawn from four directions, with no limbs, on a top-down orthographic map.
- No sourced description of the Stronghold parchment/stone panel art or the Scribe portrait's changing expression. From memory (unverified): the Scribe sits at a lectern bottom-right of the panel, and his book shows popularity with a coloured face/number.
- DK1/DK2 palette specifics (torch-lit browns/reds, gold) are not sourced.
- Against the Storm's palette per biome and season is not sourced beyond the snippets above.

## 2. How autonomous agents' thoughts, needs and moods are surfaced (what works vs. overwhelms)

### Takeaway
There are three schools. Diegetic and spoken: B&W's village-store flags and villager shouts, DK's Mentor voice. Numeric and listed: RimWorld's mood bar, threshold lines and thoughts list, and ATS's per-species Resolve with a hover breakdown. Behavioural: DK creatures sulking or rebelling, B&W's creature body morphing. Players praise "it always tells you why", and the main criticism is "it doesn't tell you how to fix it". Our hero barks plus a per-hero card match the hybrid pattern: diegetic first, numbers on demand.

### Cited Findings
**Black & White**
- "Crucial information such as resource levels and worshipper needs are indicated very neatly by flags on the village stores: the more your people need food, the higher they'll raise the flag. And they'll tell you about it too as you pass over. 'Worshippers need food!' Incessant, but effective." The author adds that casting food into the store "feels much more like being a god than hitting the 'food' button… and watching a bar replenish" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/playing-god-the-revolutionary-ai-that-made-black-and-white-still-unforgettable/)
- The Village Store shows resources and villagers' desires, and the Village Centre shows the controlling god and available miracles — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- The creature learns by reinforcement: slap to discourage, stroke to approve. It "gives visual feedback on its choices", has an alignment separate from the player's, and changes appearance with its own and its owner's alignment — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game)); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/playing-god-the-revolutionary-ai-that-made-black-and-white-still-unforgettable/)
- The creature is handed over "without explicit instruction or objectives, like the terrified parent of a newborn child" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/playing-god-the-revolutionary-ai-that-made-black-and-white-still-unforgettable/)
- B&W2 added HUD notifications reporting "the creature's state of mind, the effects of the player's actions, and village status", and lets players revisit and fine-tune learned behaviour at any time (B&W1 only allowed changes as the creature repeated actions) — [Wikipedia: Black & White 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_2)
- Two advisers (good and evil) argue with the player, and their mouths lip-sync via phoneme shapes "like graphic equalisers" — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))

**Dungeon Keeper 1/2**
- Creatures become sulky or angry from having no lair, no food or no wages, or from sharing space with natural enemies. The Mentor tells you why a creature got angry — [Dungeon Keeper Wiki: Happiness](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Happiness) (via search snippet; page 402 on direct fetch)
- Rebelling creatures may leave, defect to another keeper or the heroes, and take other creatures with them. Causes include hunger, late pay and being slapped — [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)
- The Mentor alerts you to "angry or unhappy creatures, the need for bigger rooms, the completion of spell research, items finished in the workshop, incoming attacks, or creature rebellions". Fan-transcribed lines include "Your imps are overworked." and "Angry creatures are wrecking your dungeon." — [ludo.guide: The Mentor](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/c-the-mentor); [TCRF: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://tcrf.net/Dungeon_Keeper_2) (fan-compiled; some lines unused)
- Query mode is reached from the information panel. The cursor becomes a question mark and shows creature info — [GameFAQs DK guide, SquidFetus](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/201474-dungeon-keeper-dungeon-keeper-2/faqs/81845) (via snippet). The DK1 Information Panel has a Flee toggle and a payday bar — [Dungeon Keeper Wiki: The Information Panel](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/The_Information_Panel)

**RimWorld**
- Mood bar mechanics as in Q1. The bar "chases" the target at most +12 per in-game hour rising and −8 per hour falling. The bar freezes while the pawn sleeps, and break risk pauses — [RimWorld Wiki: Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood)
- "Thoughts are a summary of a character's experiences over the last day or so" and sum to the mood target — [RimWorld Wiki: Thoughts list](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Thoughts_list); [RimWorld Wiki: Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood)
- During a mental break the colonist's name turns green on the colonist bar, and a small lightning-bolt icon sits over their portrait: yellow for non-aggressive breaks, red for aggressive ones — [RimWorld Wiki: Mental break](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mental_break)
- Patch 1.5.4062 added an option to make moods "much more visible" on the colonist bar: portraits "change color and glow when below mental break thresholds" — [RimWorld Wiki: Version/1.5.4062](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Version/1.5.4062)
- Popular mods show what players felt was missing. "Color Coded Mood Bar" tints red, orange and yellow by threshold. "Radius UI" folds the bar into a tab with slots for "critical, warning, drafted and away". "Vanilla UI+" lets you snooze alerts — [Steam Workshop: Radius UI](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3788278207); [Steam Workshop: Vanilla UI+](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3793535492); [Steam Workshop: UI tweaks collection](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1832474087)

**Against the Storm**
- Resolve (per species) rises with met needs (services, complex food) and with lower Hostility. The "Looming Darkness" storm effect gives −4 Resolve to every species, plus −4 per Hostility level (community-reported numbers) — [Against the Storm Wiki: Resolve](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Resolve); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4286936944588739641/)
- Player comment on the Resolve panel: "not the best ui but everything is here". Another says the game "will always tell you why your villagers are happy or unhappy but does a poor job explaining how to clear some of these negative effects" — Steam discussions via search: [thread 1](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4286936944588739641/), [thread 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4301571777491598797)
- Foxes are "immune to Hostility". Each village hosts three of seven species, each with its own needs (housing, food, luxuries) — [Fextralife ATS Wiki: Hostility](https://againstthestorm.wiki.fextralife.com/Against_the_Storm_Hostility); [Wikipedia: Against the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_the_Storm_(video_game))
- Inline consequence cue: the woodcutter "assign" button is green if adding a worker won't raise Hostility a level and red if it will — [Steam / casualgameguides summary](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/against-the-storm/manage-hostility-resolve-impatience)

### Inferences
- B&W's flag-height and shout pattern is the best fit for phone-first: zero chrome, readable at a glance, and personality-laden. Our guild banners or bounty flags could physically rise, sag or tatter to show need or progress, with barks as the "shout".
- RimWorld shows the cost of numeric depth. Players needed mods for colour-coded, foldable, snoozable mood displays, and Ludeon itself added glow-below-threshold in 1.5. On a phone, the colonist bar is the part worth borrowing: a portrait strip with a coloured state ring and an icon badge (bolt or similar). The thoughts list belongs in the hero card.
- ATS's "always says why, never says how" complaint suggests our hero cards should pair each mood cause with a hint ("Wants a tavern", "Fears the dark woods").
- DK's Mentor is "voice-as-alert-system", which maps to our chronicle log plus a narrator line. DK's humour (sulking creatures) makes unhappiness entertaining rather than punitive, which suits storybook tone.

### Gaps
- Not sourced: whether DK1/DK2 show icons over creature heads for sulking/angry states. From memory (unverified): DK2 creatures show small thought or anger icons, and the creature panel shows happiness with face icons.
- Not sourced: B&W's creature "Learning" scrolls and leash colours. From memory (unverified): leashes (learning, compassion, aggression) and a creature status screen in the Creature Cave/temple.
- No source on whether ATS shows need icons above individual villagers' heads (my unverified recollection is that it doesn't; needs are shown per species in panels).

## 3. How threat cycles are telegraphed

### Takeaway
The best telegraphs are layered: a persistent HUD timer or meter, a world-state change (light, rain, music), and a voice or letter cue at the moment of transition. ATS is the strongest example, with a season clock top-centre, a Hostility eye and per-season grading. RimWorld relies on colour-coded letters, while DK and Stronghold rely heavily on voice.

### Cited Findings
- **Against the Storm:** seasons cycle Drizzle (fertile), Clearance (calmest) and Storm ("thunder and lightning races across the sky, the rain is actively malicious") — [TV Tropes: Against the Storm](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Videogame/AgainstTheStorm) (fan-compiled)
- **ATS:** Hostility is a number at top centre next to the season indicator, shown as a "big red icon"/"eye". Hovering it shows what raises and lowers the score. Map effects sit right of the season indicator. Queen's Impatience is a red bar bottom-right — [Fextralife ATS Wiki: Hostility](https://againstthestorm.wiki.fextralife.com/Against_the_Storm_Hostility); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4286936944588739641/)
- **ATS** in-game tip: "High Hostility of the Forest during the storm will crush your people's Resolve… Check the hostility icon on the upper HUD to read more about Hostility sources" — [Fextralife ATS Wiki: Hostility](https://againstthestorm.wiki.fextralife.com/Against_the_Storm_Hostility)
- **ATS** reviewers note "visual and audio cues of an imminent threat" when the weather is about to turn — [Sportskeeda review](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/against-storm-review-the-perfect-blend-genres) (via snippet)
- **ATS** meta-loop: the Reputation gauge races the Queen's Impatience gauge, and gaining reputation pushes Impatience back — [Wikipedia: Against the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_the_Storm_(video_game))
- **RimWorld:** most events create a Letter on the right side of the screen. Envelope colour gives severity: blue good, grey neutral, yellow bad, red direct threat. Some events pause and open a choice window — [RimWorld Wiki: Events](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events)
- **RimWorld:** alerts and suggestions sit top-right and warn, for example, when colonists are near breaking or need rescue — [RimWorld Wiki: User interface](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/User_interface)
- **DK2:** the Mentor announces incoming attacks and rebellions (see Q2) — [ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/c-the-mentor). Heroes enter through Hero Gates — [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)

### Inferences
- Our day/night waves already resemble ATS's cycle. To stand apart, telegraph through storybook devices: a sun/moon clock framed as an illuminated-manuscript roundel, a chronicle entry ("The bells of the watchtower ring…"), and a world tint toward ink-blue dusk. The aim is to avoid a numeric Hostility meter.
- RimWorld's colour-coded envelopes are a cheap, legible taxonomy that maps well to chronicle entries. Our parchment entries could carry wax seals coloured by severity: gold good, grey neutral, amber bad, red threat. That reads as our own thing while keeping the proven four-tier system.

### Gaps
- Stronghold's invasion audio cues (e.g. the Scribe's "Your lordship, the enemy approaches!"-style warnings) and the pre-siege timer: no source found. From memory (unverified): the Scribe/advisor voices incoming-attack warnings, and invasions arrive on a scripted timer shown in some missions.
- DK1 "imp warnings" specifically: not sourced.
- RimWorld storyteller (Cassandra/Phoebe/Randy) portrait presentation and the "threat" letter animation: not sourced.
- I found no developer statement on how ATS designs storm-warning audio and visuals.

## 4. HUD layout, screen real estate, notifications and tooltips

### Takeaway
RimWorld uses the screen edges: a resource list top-left, the colonist bar top, alerts and letters right, architect and inspect bottom-left, time controls bottom-right. ATS centralises threat info top-centre and pushes the meta-meter bottom-right. Stronghold and DK use one large docked panel (bottom in Stronghold and DK2, left in DK1). B&W has almost no HUD. Tooltip-on-hover is everyone's depth layer, which is exactly what phones lack.

### Cited Findings
- **RimWorld layout:** top-left Resources list (transparent, scrollable, stockpiled items only, optional categories); top colonist bar (names, appearance, status icons, grouped by settlement or caravan, left-click jumps, right-click world map); top-right alerts and the Learning helper (which replaced the "yellow pop-up messages and yellow learning alerts"); bottom-left Architect menu and inspect pane (or cell info: roof, floor, filth, light level); bottom menu bar; bottom-right overlay toggles (zones, beauty, home area, roofs) and time controls (pause, 1x, 3x, 5x) with day/hour — [RimWorld Wiki: User interface](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/User_interface)
- **RimWorld:** mood lives in the Needs tab of the pawn inspect pane — [RimWorld Wiki: Mental break](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mental_break)
- **DK:** the "entire user interface is contained within the large panel at the bottom of the screen" (DK2-era guide; snippet cut off) — [GameFAQs, SquidFetus](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/201474-dungeon-keeper-dungeon-keeper-2/faqs/81845). DK1 used a left bar with a minimap top-left — [Computer Hope](https://www.computerhope.com:443/games/games/dungeon.htm)
- **DK2:** the interface "was redesigned more than ten times based on feedback so that the public could understand it" — [Wikipedia: Dungeon Keeper 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2)
- **War for the Overworld:** a reviewer criticised the missing minimap. The unit panel was later optimised from 5 ms to 0.1 ms render time — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/war-for-the-overworld-hands-on-impressions/amp); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/articles/20840/)
- **Stronghold:** "the big number on the scribe's book is popularity; 50+ means peasants will come and any lower means they will leave", and the fraction at the bottom of the book is population — [Steam: Stronghold Crusader HD discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/40970/discussions/0/3147430975647502052/)
- **Stronghold DE:** the Scribe gained "a small arrow on the popularity status" showing its trend — [Strategy and Wargaming review](https://strategyandwargaming.com/2023/11/18/stronghold-definitive-edition-review/)
- **Stronghold fear factor (Crusader DE):** a flower button in the Town tab holds "Good Things" and a noose button holds "Bad Things". Four Good Things make one Good Level, while each Bad Thing makes one Bad Level. Levels are capped at 5 and cancel each other out. In the original Crusader the scale is ±5, each step being ±1 popularity — [TheGamer fear factor guide](https://www.thegamer.com/stronghold-crusader-definitive-edition-fear-factor-guide/); [HeavenGames: Popularity in Crusader](https://stronghold.heavengames.com/sc/strategy/popularity/)
- **ATS:** the top-centre cluster is season indicator, Hostility and map effects, with Impatience bottom-right (see Q3). Tooltips are the main teaching tool: new players are told to hover "to get tooltips with information for you to read". Players call the learning curve steep, "a huge knowledge gap" — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4039231510639136534)
- **ATS accessibility:** "some of the text is initially small, though it can be adjusted in size, and some of it doesn't contrast with the background" — [Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Against+the+Storm)
- **B&W:** miracle selection happens at the Temple or Village Centre, or by drawing gestures. Gold scrolls start major story events and silver scrolls give side tasks; these are in-world markers, not HUD — [Wikipedia: Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))

### Inferences
- Our fan wheel bottom-right takes the slot RimWorld and ATS use for time and meta controls. Threat and day state should go top-centre, which matches ATS convention, and alerts or chronicle should go along one side.
- Stronghold's Scribe is the best precedent for "a character is the meter". A Realm herald or chronicler portrait whose expression and quill show realm mood would be both diegetic and phone-friendly, and distinct from Stronghold if styled as a storybook illumination rather than a sprite at a desk.
- Hover tooltips carry the depth in RimWorld and ATS. On touch, a tap-to-pin card (our info cards) plus long-press must replace hover, and every chronicle entry should be tappable to jump to its subject, like RimWorld's letters and colonist bar.

### Gaps
- No pixel or percentage measurements of screen real estate for any title.
- The Game UI Database (gameuidatabase.com) wasn't fetched in this pass, and it would give screenshot-level layout per title.
- RimWorld tooltip conventions (mood breakdown on hover) aren't sourced, though widely known.

## 5. Mobile and console adaptations

### Takeaway
RimWorld Console Edition is the most instructive: a centre reticle, UI in four corners mapped to shoulder buttons, radial menus, and "nothing more than three button presses away". ATS consoles (June 2025) got a "fully reworked" gamepad UX by QLOC. Stronghold Kingdoms mobile claimed a full touch redesign but drew "clunky" complaints. DK mobile (2014) is remembered for timer and gem monetisation, not UI.

### Cited Findings
- **RimWorld Console Edition (Double Eleven, July 2022):** "Instead of using a mouse-like cursor, you have a large circle in the middle of the screen that you use to highlight things in the world, while the UI lives in the corners, with each corner assigned to a different shoulder button… you're never more than three button presses away." The UI redesign was so heavy that it "uses a different technology stack to the PC game". Verdict: "Despite the UI being great, it unavoidably gets awkward at times" (8.1/10) — [DualShockers, Robert Zak](https://www.dualshockers.com/rimworld-console-edition-review-ps4-xbox-one/)

## 7. One-paragraph visual "signature" per game (for differentiation)

### Takeaway
Each signature below is a synthesis of the cited findings above. Where it relies on my unverified recollection, that is flagged.

### Cited Findings
- See the citations in Q1 to Q4. The paragraphs below are synthesis and are listed under Inferences.

### Inferences
- **Dungeon Keeper 1/2:** A subterranean, torch-lit, darkly comic world seen from a top-down, tilted 3D camera. The player is a giant clawed Hand of Evil that grabs, drops and slaps minions. A sardonic Mentor voice is the alert system, and creatures show their mood by sulking, rebelling or leaving. Possession lets you drop into first person. The palette (my recollection, unverified) is earthy browns, lava reds and gold, inside a heavy stone-and-gold UI frame (left bar in DK1, bottom panel in DK2). War for the Overworld keeps the same grammar with modern rendering.
- **Black & White 1/2:** A lush, sun-drenched island world with a fully free 3D god camera. The UI is almost absent: a magician's hand, mouse gestures for miracles, and in-world gold and silver scrolls for quests. Village-store flags rise with need while villagers shout requests in regional British accents. The whole world reskins with your morality (bright temples or menacing ones, haloed or fanged creature, even the hand). The giant pet creature is a living status bar whose body shape and colour show what it has learned. B&W2 added conventional HUD notifications and toolbars.
- **Stronghold series:** A sunlit isometric 2D medieval countryside with many small, busy peasant sprites and stone castles you assemble piece by piece. A large docked bottom panel holds building categories and, crucially, the Scribe: a human narrator whose book shows popularity (50+ = immigration) with a trend arrow added in DE. Morality is a literal flower-or-noose "fear factor" lever. Heavy voice-acted advisor barks carry personality. Critics have found the panel art functional to "bland".
- **RimWorld:** A top-down orthographic, flat-shaded, minimalist map with simple pawns and a muted palette (art details unverified). It cedes drama to text: a portrait colonist bar along the top, colour-coded letters and alerts down the right edge (blue, grey, yellow, red), and a dense inspect pane with a blue mood bar, white target triangle and three threshold ticks. Mental breaks show as a green name plus a yellow or red lightning bolt. Information is total and on demand, which is why mods and 1.5 added glow and colour-coding for at-a-glance reading.
- **Against the Storm:** A painterly or cel-shaded dark-fantasy forest clearing (art direction cites *The Way of Kings*' storm imagery), recoloured by a three-season rain cycle: Drizzle, Clearance, and Storm with lightning and a malicious downpour. Each season has its own post-process grade, and nights are dim. The HUD centres a season clock and a red Hostility "eye" top-centre, with Queen's Impatience bottom-right and per-species Resolve in panels. Hover tooltips always explain "why", though players find the text small and onboarding steep.
- **Differentiation space for Hold the Hall Realm:** Every one of these uses either a disembodied god hand (DK, B&W), a functional modern HUD (RimWorld, ATS), or a narrator portrait (Stronghold). None uses an illuminated-manuscript or storybook framing where the chronicle itself is the primary alert surface. None shows agent mood through painted banner or flag states on an isometric phone screen, though B&W's store flags come closest. Heroes acting on their own with trait-driven barks are closest to DK creatures plus RimWorld thoughts. Rendering them as speech scrolls or ink vignettes, rather than bars or bolts, would be distinctive.

### Gaps
- The palettes for DK and RimWorld here are my own unverified characterisations, and screenshot analysis would confirm them.
