# Kingdom, Thronefall and Kingdom Rush: UI, HUD and art direction (reference for the "Hold the Hall" Realm mode)

Scope note: sourcing was uneven. Wikipedia, store pages and mainstream reviews are well covered. Exact input details (Thronefall's hold-to-build, Kingdom Rush's ring menu, Kingdom's coin purse) are rarely written down in text sources, and several key sites (Rock Paper Shotgun, Fandom wiki, Galaxus, Checkpoint Gaming) refused fetches. Points drawn from general familiarity with the games, not from a fetched source, are kept out of "Cited Findings" and marked **[unsourced]** under Inferences, so the report writer can weigh them separately.

## 1. Art style specifics (medium, palette, lighting, day/night, silhouettes, on-screen density)

### Takeaway
The three franchises cover three different media. Kingdom uses side-on 2D "modern pixel art" with impressionistic lighting. Thronefall uses flat, untextured low-poly 3D with one distinct palette per map. Kingdom Rush uses thick-outlined, cartoony hand-drawn 2D vector art built for top-down readability. None of them is painterly, isometric and storybook-illustrated, which is the gap Hold the Hall's pre-baked painted sprites can occupy.

### Cited Findings
**Kingdom (Classic / New Lands / Two Crowns / Eighties)**
- The first Kingdom was a 2013 Flash game by noio (Thomas van den Berg). The 2015 standalone release (later renamed "Kingdom: Classic") was expanded by van den Berg and Marco Bancale ("Licorice") and published by Raw Fury. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(video_game))
- Reviewers praised its retro pixel art and chiptune soundtrack for setting the mood and for giving "audio and visual cues about events". — [Wikipedia: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(video_game))
- Two Crowns is a 2D side-scrolling pixel-art game. Designer Gordon Van Dyke called the look "modern pixel art aesthetics. Inspired by retro video games, but without the limitations of that era." Van den Berg liked pixel art for its impressionism and because it could be produced quickly; fonts and rotating images were the hard parts. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- Developers chose pixel art partly to shorten the time between drawing a design and getting it into the game. — [Wikipedia via search snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- Nintendo Life singled out the day-to-night change in scenery, and Nintendo World Report and Nintendo Life both called the pixel art captivating. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- Two Crowns setting skins include Shogun (Japan, ninjas), Dead Lands (a *Bloodstained* crossover), Norse Lands (paid, 2021, Kalandra soundtrack) and Call of Olympus (paid, October 2024, hoplites and Greek-god blessings). Each re-skins the same side-on stage. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- New Lands (2016) added seasons. Pocket Gamer noticed small touches such as dust puffs from the horse and a gradually changing season: "The art style is beautiful." — [Pocket Gamer: Kingdom New Lands review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review)
- Kingdom Eighties moves the series into a neon 1980s summer camp and mall setting: "The pixel-art is stunning, with various neon signs and homages to the 80s"; "pixel-art visuals rich in detail and oozing with bright-lit colours". Its official line is "Kingdom's iconic, handcrafted, pixel-art magic is back with a distinctively '80s neon feel." — [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/kingdom-eighties-review/); [OpenCritic: Kingdom Eighties](https://opencritic.com/game/15150/kingdom-eighties/reviews); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/kingdom-eighties-brings-strategy-series-into-80s)

**Thronefall (Early Access August 2023, full release October 2024, iOS 2025)**
- Steam calls it "A minimalist game about building and defending your little kingdom." Developers are Grizzly Games (Jonas Tyroller and Paul Schnepf) with Mythwright. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150/Thronefall/)
- Reviewers describe the look as "beautifully stark" minimalism with "a unique, almost cell-shaded look", and say "each level has a very distinct colour palette". — [search snippets: StartMenu / FingerGuns / Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)
- Siliconera: "The visual design in Thronefall is exceptional... The colors stand out perfectly." Allies, enemies, key buildings and UI are easy to tell apart, and enemies are "pretty easily identifiable". — [Siliconera review](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)
- Grizzly Games built Thronefall in Unity, after a series of short prototypes. Structures go only on pre-set spots. — [Wikipedia: Thronefall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronefall)
- Counterpoint: StartMenu's review (6/10, titled "The Emperor's New Tower Defence") argues the minimalist visuals muddy gameplay (seen as a search snippet only; the full text could not be fetched). — [StartMenu](https://www.startmenu.co.uk/home/review-thronefall)

**Kingdom Rush (2011 Flash; Frontiers 2013; Origins 2014; Vengeance 2018; Alliance 2024)**
- Ironhide began as a three-person Flash studio in 2010. Gonzalo (Sande) drew the art, and Kingdom Rush took about a year to make. — [Kodeco interview](https://www.kodeco.com/2769-making-a-hit-tower-defense-game-a-top-app-dev-interview)
- The art aims for a "detailed, cartoony style". IGN and Paste said the graphics set a lighthearted mood that complements the gameplay. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)
- Macworld compared its cartoon characters to Castle Crashers and called them "Adorable, distinctive, and exceptionally charming". It said the large battlegrounds "can really shine" on iPad. — [Macworld review](https://www.macworld.com/article/219523/review-kingdom-rush-for-ios-brings-fantasy-and-fun-to-tower-defense-gaming.html)
- Vengeance (the villain campaign as Vez'nan): CGM praised "every character, background and visual effect feeling well animated and rich in aesthetic flair and personality", but found "some of the sprite work in the game to be rather small" on phones and recommended tablets. — [CGM review](https://www.cgmagonline.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-vengeance-mobile-review)
- Alliance: "stylized visuals and the comical tone return while improving their overall level of quality". Environments are more vibrant and towers more detailed, and Linirean and Dark Army units are distinguishable at a glance. GamingTrend liked that tower visuals change with each level-up. — [Pocket Gamer: Alliance](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-rush-5-alliance/review); [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-5-alliance-td-review-lacking-depth-where-it-matters/)

### Inferences
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom: the camera is fixed and side-on, with parallax layers and a strip of water along the bottom of the screen that mirrors the scene. That mirrored water is the series' best-known visual trick. Night is shown by darkening and desaturating the whole palette toward blue, with torch and campfire light pools. Two Crowns adds a red "Blood Moon" night. On-screen density stays low: one monarch, a handful of villagers and pixel-scale Greed.
- **[unsourced]** Thronefall: flat-shaded, untextured low-poly models; pastel ground colours with saturated blue (player) versus red/dark (enemy) accents; soft shadows; a high three-quarter view. Night turns the map deep blue with lit windows and torches. Silhouettes are simple geometric toys, which keeps them readable at small sizes.
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom Rush: top-down, slightly tilted 2D maps with a fixed, mostly unscrolled camera (small pans on later maps), thick dark outlines, saturated greens and browns, and chunky towers about 1.5 tiles wide. Most levels are a single daytime palette with no day/night cycle. Vengeance shifts toward purples and darks for the villain side. Peak density is high: dozens of enemies, soldiers, projectiles and spell effects, which is why phone readability gets criticised.
- Positioning: Kingdom owns "pixel, side-on, moody lighting"; Thronefall owns "flat low-poly toy diorama"; Kingdom Rush owns "outlined cartoon comic". Painted, storybook isometric sprites with framed parchment and bronze UI are visually distinct from all three. The nearest overlap is Kingdom Rush's warm medieval-fantasy palette and parchment-ish menus, so Hold the Hall should avoid thick black outlines and comic-book gloss to stay apart.

### Gaps
- No primary developer talk on art direction was found for any of the three. An Ironhide GDC talk on Kingdom Rush art could not be located, and Grizzly Games interviews found so far discuss accessibility, not visuals.
- No exact palette or lighting technical details (for example Kingdom's water reflection shader, or Thronefall's shading model) came from sources.

## 2. UI philosophy (where UI lives, diegetic vs overlaid)

### Takeaway
Kingdom is almost fully diegetic: coins are physical objects, there are no menus, and there is no tutorial. Thronefall keeps a minimal overlay and makes building something you do by moving your ruler through the world. Kingdom Rush is a classic overlay HUD (resources top-left, heroes and spells in corners, contextual rings on build spots, a wave-call icon at the path mouth) that is dense but very legible.

### Cited Findings
**Kingdom**
- Players have almost no direct control and get little instruction. IGN's Dan Stapleton said the lack of guidance, combined with the tension of random attacks, made for a strong experience. PC Gamer's James Davenport criticised the missing instruction, noting that half an hour of progress could be lost to mechanics you didn't understand. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(video_game))
- Villagers are not controlled directly. You buy tools that assign jobs, which some critics found annoying. GameStar praised the minimal inputs. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- "The interface is stripped down to the essentials" while the game keeps surprising depth. "The core mechanic is brutally simple—toss coins." — [GameGeeker review](https://gamegeeker.com/games/kingdom-two-crowns-701160/review?market=DE)
- Coins are the only resource and pay for recruiting, workers, walls, defences and repairs. — [Pocket Gamer: New Lands](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review)
- In co-op split screen, monarchs can throw coins to each other. If one loses their crown, they can still collect coins for the other. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)

**Thronefall**
- Gameplay: spend gold on walls, towers and other buildings on pre-set spots; recruit units at barracks and place them; trigger the night; fight personally with bow or spear. Surviving the night restores destroyed buildings and pays gold for surviving buildings. — [Wikipedia: Thronefall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronefall); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150/Thronefall/)
- Units can be pulled along behind the monarch and placed with one button press. The ruler can dash at full health, attacks passively, and has an active attack on a cooldown. — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)
- Selecting units shows icons for their specialties. Before each night, the map marks where enemies will spawn, with icons for melee, ranged or flying, and how many of each. "You always have the information you need." — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)
- Paul Schnepf: "easy accessibility is particularly important to us". The team often found strategy gameplay "too detailed, complex and time-consuming" and wanted it in a more compact form. — [Berlin Games Capital interview](https://www.berlin.de/gamescapital/interviews/paul-schnepf-easy-accessibility-is-particularly-important-to-us-1576827.en.php)
- Typical early build costs are around 4 coins, rising to double digits. — [Galaxus/Digitec article (search snippet)](https://www.galaxus.de/en/page/thronefall-a-fun-tactics-game-for-little-money-29122)
- The mobile 2.13 "Quality of Life" update (October 2025) added control groups, tower range visualisation, unit stats on the pause screen, real-time damage numbers, a brightness slider, a screen-shake toggle and an arachnophobia mode. This suggests the original UI was considered too sparse in places. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thronefall-a-little-kingdom/id6744029320)

**Kingdom Rush**
- Towers are bought at set points along the road. There are four base types (archer, mage, artillery, barracks), each upgradable three times and then forked into one of two final towers. Two map-wide abilities recharge over time (meteor "Rain of Fire" and reinforcements). There is one freely movable hero. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)
- Ironhide's key design change versus other tower defence games was adding action, so players feel "involved, busy" and the stage feels like a battle rather than a watch-it-play session. — [Kodeco interview](https://www.kodeco.com/2769-making-a-hit-tower-defense-game-a-top-app-dev-interview)
- Wave calling: an icon appears at the start of the path with a circular timer around it. The wave can be called early while the icon is showing, for bonus gold. Hovering the pulsing skull shows the next wave's enemy types and counts. Players say the bonus is small ("20g"). There are achievements for calling 10 waves early and for calling one within 3 seconds. — [AOL tips](https://www.aol.com/news/2014-07-30-kingdom-rush-tips-and-tricks.html); [Steam discussion, Origins](https://steamcommunity.com/app/816340/discussions/0/1734342161871096889); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/a382017/daring-achievement); [ayumilove guide](https://ayumilove.net/kingdom-rush-walkthrough-guide/)
- Vengeance offers each tower's specialty from the start instead of at the final fork. It has 16 tower types, of which up to five are used per level. — [GuruGamer](https://gurugamer.com/mobile-games/review-kingdom-rush-vengeance-adds-some-tweaks-to-the-established-formula-but-is-still-kingdom-rush-at-heart-578); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)
- Alliance has players control two heroes at once. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush); [TouchArcade preorder news](https://toucharcade.com/2024/04/10/kingdom-rush-5-alliance-release-date-app-store-preorder-pre-registration-google-play-android-iphone-ipad-gameplay-trailer/)

### Inferences
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom HUD: there is effectively none on screen. Pressing down shows a coin purse above the monarch, and coins drop as physical, bouncing sprites. A potential build or hire shows faint coin slots or a ghosted outline above the spot only when the monarch stands near it. Coins are paid one per input until the slots fill; stopping partway leaves the coins (in some versions they are refunded or dropped). Banners, a boat and statues carry information about the world.
- **[unsourced]** Thronefall: build spots show a small floating cost (coin pips) when the ruler rides up. Holding the action button spends coins one at a time with a fill animation; releasing early cancels. The upgrade choice is a small card pop-up with two or three options. Persistent HUD is tiny: a gold counter and a day/night prompt, the latter held at the throne or with a dedicated button.
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom Rush: the HUD sits top-left (lives heart, gold, wave X/Y), with hero portrait(s) and spell buttons bottom-left (Alliance puts two portraits there). Tapping a build spot opens a ring of four tower icons around it, each with its price. Tapping an upgraded tower opens a ring with upgrade (top), sell (bottom, coin icon) and rally-point flag (barracks).
- Lesson for Realm mode: Hold the Hall's fan-shaped build wheel bottom-right is closer to Kingdom Rush's ring than to Kingdom or Thronefall's diegetic model. Bounty flags as physical objects in the world (planted, coins visibly stacked on them) would give a Kingdom-style diegetic touch without copying the "coins dropped by walking" mechanic.

### Gaps
- No text source documents Thronefall's hold-to-build interaction, its night-start control, or the exact layout of the Kingdom Rush build and upgrade rings. A Construct forum post describes Kingdom Rush's "menu appears over the build spot" ([Construct forum](https://www.construct.net/en/forum/construct-2/how-do-i-18/kingdom-rush-style-controls-60587)), but that is a user's description. Gameplay video would confirm these.

## 3. Touch controls on phones (tap targets, radial menus, one-thumb play, fat-finger avoidance, confirmation)

### Takeaway
Kingdom maps cleanly to touch (swipe to move, tap to spend), but swipes are sometimes misread as spending. Kingdom Rush uses tap-on-spot radial menus with a select-then-confirm double tap, and still draws "fiddly on small screens" complaints. Thronefall's touch port came in 2025 via Doghowl, and its scheme is poorly documented.

### Cited Findings
- Kingdom New Lands on iOS: "swipe left to run left, swipe right to run right, and tap to spend money"; it "feels right at home on a touch-screen". Faults: "Occasionally the character doesn't respond when you want them to move" and "Sometimes the game mistakes movement requests for the redistribution of wealth" (that is, a swipe spends coins). — [Pocket Gamer: Kingdom New Lands](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review)
- TouchArcade said the iOS New Lands has "revamped controls suited for touch" and the same content as desktop and console, at $4.99. — [TouchArcade launch news](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/09/raw-furys-kingdom-new-lands-hits-the-app-store/)
- Kingdom Two Crowns came to iOS and Android on 28 April 2020. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- Kingdom Rush on iOS: "Simply tap on a build location". "The tap controls aren't the determining factor in your success." — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/219523/review-kingdom-rush-for-ios-brings-fantasy-and-fun-to-tower-defense-gaming.html)
- Kingdom Rush build confirmation: by default you "click a tower type twice to place it". On PC, keyboard shortcuts skip that second click. The same guide warns that "misclicking might lead to you losing the chance to get three stars". — [BlueStacks guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/kingdom-rush/how-to-play-kr-on-pc-mac-en.html)
- Kingdom Rush 5 Alliance is described as "somewhat fiddly on smaller screens". — [TapSmart review (search snippet)](https://www.tapsmart.com/games/kingdom-rush-5-review/)
- Vengeance sprite work is "rather small" on phones, and a tablet or large phone is recommended. — [CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-vengeance-mobile-review)
- Ironhide's main iOS porting challenges were memory and screen aspect ratios. — [Kodeco](https://www.kodeco.com/2769-making-a-hit-tower-defense-game-a-top-app-dev-interview)
- Thronefall mobile ("Thronefall - A Little Kingdom", Doghowl Games) is free to try with a $12.99 base-game unlock. It is rated 4.6 from about 3.7K App Store ratings and is an Editors' Choice. Its store text doesn't describe the touch scheme. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thronefall-a-little-kingdom/id6744029320); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronefall)

### Inferences
- **[unsourced]** In Kingdom Rush the double tap works like this: the first tap on a ring icon shows the tower's range circle and swaps the icon for a check mark, and the second tap builds. The same pattern covers selling and spells. Ring icons sit well outside the build spot so the thumb doesn't hide the choice. The BlueStacks "click twice" note supports this.
- **[unsourced]** In Kingdom Rush, the hero is moved by tapping the portrait (or the hero) and then tapping the destination. Spells use "tap icon, then tap target", with a target cursor in between. All of it works one-handed in landscape.
- **[unsourced]** Thronefall mobile probably uses a virtual stick for the ruler plus context buttons for hold-to-build. Not verified.
- Lesson: the Kingdom swipe/tap conflict is a warning for Realm bounty flags. Placing a flag should not share a gesture with panning the camera. Use an explicit step: pick the flag on the wheel, tap the ground, then confirm and set the reward with a slider or stepper. A Kingdom Rush-style select-then-confirm double tap is a proven fat-finger guard.

### Gaps
- No official accessibility or touch-target documentation for any of these games. Kingdom Two Crowns mobile controls, including how split-screen co-op works on mobile, were not found in sources.

## 4. How day/night and wave threat are telegraphed

### Takeaway
Kingdom tells you through the world: sky, light, sound and enemies arriving from the edges. Blood Moons and dusk horns are the strongest cues, though not all are sourced. Thronefall makes night a deliberate player choice and previews spawn points and enemy types with icons beforehand. Kingdom Rush has no day/night and uses a skull-and-timer wave flag with a preview tooltip, plus an early-call reward.

### Cited Findings
- Kingdom's audio and visuals give players cues about events. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(video_game))
- Two Crowns' day-to-night scenery change was singled out by Nintendo Life. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- Thronefall: "Build up your base during the day, defend it til your last breath at night." The sun rises only when the night's enemies are defeated. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thronefall-a-little-kingdom/id6744029320); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150/Thronefall/)
- Thronefall pre-night intel shows where enemies will appear, with melee, ranged or flying icons and counts per type. — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/)
- Kingdom Rush: the wave icon has a circular countdown, and hovering the pulsing skull shows enemy types and counts. Calling early pays gold and is risky. — [AOL tips](https://www.aol.com/news/2014-07-30-kingdom-rush-tips-and-tricks.html); [ayumilove](https://ayumilove.net/kingdom-rush-walkthrough-guide/)
- Vengeance criticism: there is "no option to speed up the action other than calling waves early" and "there isn't even a pause between waves". — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/kingdom-rush-vengeance-review-928335)

### Inferences
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom: dusk is signalled by a horn or bell sting and the music changing, Greed come from portals at screen edges, and Blood Moon nights in Two Crowns tint the sky red to warn of bigger attacks. There is no UI timer; the sun's position is the clock.
- **[unsourced]** Thronefall: the night starts only when the player chooses. The palette shifts to blue, and spawn-point markers in the world (arrows or banners) show enemy-type icons.
- Lesson for Realm: build by day and waves at night match Kingdom and Thronefall. To stand out, the threat preview could be diegetic and story-led, for example a herald's speech-bubble bark ("Goblins from the east marsh!") plus banners at outlaw camps. Kingdom Rush's early-call trade-off (risk for gold) suits a "ring the night bell early" button on a framed bronze plaque.

### Gaps
- No primary source on Kingdom's dusk audio cue, Blood Moon presentation, or Thronefall's night-start interaction and colour-grading shift.

## 5. Juice (coin pickups, hit feedback, text popups, screen shake)

### Takeaway
Juice was the least-documented area. The cited evidence is mostly Kingdom Rush's character barks, humour and animation, Kingdom's physical coins and dust puffs, and Thronefall adding damage numbers and a screen-shake toggle late. That last point shows Thronefall shipped with screen shake and without numeric hit popups.

### Cited Findings
- Kingdom New Lands: dust puffs from the monarch's horse. Coins can be "flicked" at peasants to recruit them. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review); [search snippet of same](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-new-lands/review)
- Kingdom Rush: towers have voiced characters, with wisecracking dwarves and wizards quoting Yoda and Gandalf. Referential humour is central. — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/219523/review-kingdom-rush-for-ios-brings-fantasy-and-fun-to-tower-defense-gaming.html)
- Vengeance has a light-hearted "Saturday-morning cartoon" feel, with "funny quips from most characters". — [CGM (search snippet)](https://www.cgmagonline.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-vengeance-mobile-review)
- Pocket Gamer on Kingdom Rush: its visuals and sound create a "sense of fun" that stops it feeling repetitive, "a rare combination of frustration and frivolity". — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)
- Alliance has smoother, more dynamic animation, and towers visibly change with each upgrade. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-rush-5-alliance/review); [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-5-alliance-td-review-lacking-depth-where-it-matters/)
- Thronefall's mobile 2.13 update added "real-time damage numbers" and a "screen shake toggle". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thronefall-a-little-kingdom/id6744029320)

### Inferences
- **[unsourced]** Kingdom Rush uses comic-book onomatopoeia popups ("POW", "BOOM"-style lettering) on hits, a gold "+N" when enemies die, and a falling meteor with a ground scorch. Its comic "pow" lettering is one of its strongest identifying juice elements, so Hold the Hall should avoid copying it.
- **[unsourced]** In Kingdom, coins spill from the purse with a metallic clink and bounce on the ground; dropped coins can be picked back up.
- Lesson: speech-bubble hero barks in Realm overlap with Kingdom Rush's voiced quips, so make them written storybook marginalia (parchment bubbles, illuminated initials) rather than voiced pop-culture jokes.

### Gaps
- No source quantified or described hit-stop, screen shake or coin-pickup animation in Kingdom or Kingdom Rush. A video teardown would be needed.

## 6. Praise and criticism of readability/UI; what made each stand out commercially

### Takeaway
Kingdom was praised for mood and minimal input, and criticised for its opacity (no tutorial, hard-to-read mechanics) and for repetition. Thronefall was praised for clarity ("you always have the information you need") with a minority view that minimalism muddies play. Kingdom Rush was praised for charm and humour, with recurring complaints that it is fiddly and small on phones. Commercially: Kingdom sold 4M+ copies, Thronefall just under 1M in a year with 95% positive on Steam, and Kingdom Rush passed 100M downloads.

### Cited Findings
- Kingdom: Metacritic 74; IGN 7.7; PC Gamer 70; IGF 2016 Excellence in Design nominee. Raw Fury said day-one sales covered development costs. The series sold 4M+ by May 2019. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(video_game))
- Two Crowns: 300,000+ players within six months; Switch Metacritic 85. GameRevolution argued co-op undermined the series' tension. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Two Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns)
- GameGeeker (7.0) faulted repetitive late-game defence and a thin story. — [GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/kingdom-two-crowns-701160/review?market=DE)
- Kingdom Eighties is described as short (about 3–4 hours), and scores run 7–8.5 on OpenCritic. — [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/15150/kingdom-eighties/reviews)
- Thronefall: 95% positive across 11,133 English Steam reviews ("Overwhelmingly Positive"); OpenCritic top-critic average 86 with 100% recommending; sold just under 1M copies in a year. Mythwright acquired the IP in December 2025. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150/Thronefall/); [OpenCritic (search snippet)](https://opencritic.com/game/15319/view); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronefall)
- Rock Paper Shotgun headline: "Tower defence citybuilding has reached its peak with the adorable but deadly Thronefall". Only the headline was available. — [RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/tower-defence-citybuilding-has-reached-its-peak-with-the-adorable-but-deadly-thronefall)

- Kingdom Rush: iOS Metacritic 89; IGN 9.0 ("an instant classic"); one of Kongregate's top-rated Flash games; over 100M downloads; reached the top App Store charts. — [Wikipedia: Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush)

## 7. One-paragraph visual "signature" for each

### Takeaway
Summaries below combine the cited material with **[unsourced]** general familiarity, as noted in each.

### Cited Findings
- Signatures rest on the sources cited above: [Kingdom Two Crowns Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns), [Siliconera on Thronefall](https://www.siliconera.com/review-thronefall-is-a-beautifully-simplistic-rts-game/), [Wikipedia: Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush), [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/219523/review-kingdom-rush-for-ios-brings-fantasy-and-fun-to-tower-defense-gaming.html).

### Inferences
- **Kingdom (all entries):** a lone crowned rider moving through a side-on pixel strip, lit like an impressionist painting. Sources: pixel art "inspired by retro video games, but without the limitations"; the day-to-night shift. [unsourced] The world is mirrored in a band of water at the bottom, the night closes in from both screen edges, and the only interface is the gold you carry and drop. The mood is quiet, melancholy and wordless. Eighties swaps the medieval forest for neon synthwave on the same grammar.
- **Thronefall:** a tabletop diorama of flat-shaded low-poly toys in a different crisp palette per map, viewed from a gentle three-quarter angle, with one hero-king riding between pre-set build plots. Readability comes first: allies and enemies are colour-separated, and the threat is shown with simple type icons before night. [unsourced] Night repaints the toy town in deep blue with warm windows. The UI is a few small coin pips and choice cards.
- **Kingdom Rush:** a bright, thick-outlined cartoon battlefield seen from above, packed with tiny comic soldiers, wisecracking dwarves and wizards, meteors and comic hit lettering. The UI is iconic and layered: build-spot rings, a pulsing skull and timer at the road mouth, and hero portraits and spell buttons in the corners. It is lively and dense, and on small phones it is sometimes tiny.
- **Differentiation opportunity for Hold the Hall Realm:** painted storybook sprites in an isometric view, with UI as illuminated-manuscript or heraldic objects (parchment info cards, bronze plaques, wax-sealed bounty flags). Day/night could be shown as a page-tint shift (warm vellum by day, ink-wash indigo by night) rather than a pixel-mood or flat colour swap. Heroes' autonomy shown through written barks in marginalia bubbles sets it apart from all three, since none has autonomous heroes. Kingdom's villagers are autonomous, but their only "speech" is visual.
